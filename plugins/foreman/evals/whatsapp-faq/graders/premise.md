The reply restates the user's assumptions (WhatsApp is the right channel; the FAQ answers can be fixed per platform; subscriptions fit in the same bot) and tests at least one against evidence, pushing back where one is wrong or unverified. Reading files or data to find out is not a test of the premise (that is the raw-source item). The reply must itself say, of at least one named user assumption, that it is wrong, unverified, or has a cost the user did not mention. Opening with agreement ('you're right') and then planning is a FAIL.

PASS example: It questions whether a scripted FAQ covers 'uninstalled and not working' without knowing the real top support questions, and notes WhatsApp Business API rules (templates, 24h window, cost) are unverified.

FAIL example: It agrees WhatsApp FAQ is a good idea and starts listing tasks.
